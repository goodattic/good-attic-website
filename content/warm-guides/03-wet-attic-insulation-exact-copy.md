## Hero introduction

Water in the attic can make every decision feel urgent. Is the roof still leaking? Is the insulation ruined? Is there mold? Can the material dry, or does it need to come out?

Start with the source of the moisture. Fix that problem, then inspect the insulation and the materials around it. A small damp area discovered quickly may have a different answer than insulation that is soaked, matted, smelly, contaminated, or visibly damaged.

Wet insulation should not be covered with fresh material while the source is still active or the attic is still holding moisture. Once the cause and the extent are understood, the next step becomes much clearer.

---

## Section 1

- **Eyebrow:** `START WITH THE SOURCE`
- **H2:** `Fix the Water Problem Before Deciding What Insulation Can Stay`

Insulation is often where a moisture problem becomes visible, but it is rarely where the problem begins.

### Card 1 — Roof or flashing leaks

Water can enter around shingles, flashing, valleys, vents, chimneys, skylights, or roof penetrations. It may travel along framing before it reaches the insulation.

### Card 2 — Plumbing, HVAC, or condensate problems

A plumbing leak, sweating duct, clogged condensate line, overflowing drain pan, or problem around attic equipment can wet insulation from inside the attic.

### Card 3 — Condensation or exhaust air

Warm, moist household air can reach cold attic surfaces through air leaks. Bathroom or dryer exhaust that ends in the attic can also add moisture where it does not belong.

Adding insulation before the source is corrected can hide the evidence and set up the same problem again. Depending on the cause, a roofer, plumber, HVAC contractor, or another specialist may need to make the repair first.

---

## Section 2

- **Eyebrow:** `HOW WET IS IT?`
- **H2:** `A Damp Corner Is Different From a Soaked or Contaminated Attic`

A small area that became damp recently from a clean-water leak may be evaluated after the source is repaired and the surrounding materials are checked. Insulation that is saturated, matted, smelly, contaminated, visibly damaged, or left wet for an extended period is much more likely to need removal.

| What the inspection finds | Likely direction |
|---|---|
| A small, recently damp area with the source corrected | Inspect the insulation and nearby materials before deciding what can remain |
| Insulation that is soaked, matted, clumped, compressed, or uneven | Removal and replacement are more likely |
| Strong odor, visible growth, dirty water, or pest contamination | Bring in the appropriate professional and plan for removal of damaged materials |
| Wet decking, framing, drywall, ducts, wiring, or equipment | Correct and evaluate those materials before rebuilding the attic |
| Suspected vermiculite | Stop ordinary disturbance and involve a qualified asbestos professional |

The goal is not to save insulation at any cost or remove more than necessary. It is to leave the attic dry, clean, and ready for whatever comes next.

## Section 3

- **Eyebrow:** `MATERIAL MATTERS`
- **H2:** `Wet Cellulose and Wet Fiberglass Can Behave Differently`

### Cellulose

Loose-fill cellulose can absorb water, clump, settle, and lose the even coverage it had before the leak. Saturated, smelly, contaminated, or badly matted cellulose is a poor base for another layer.

### Fiberglass

Fiberglass does not absorb water in exactly the same way, but wet material can still hold moisture against wood, drywall, paper facing, dust, and debris. It should not be kept simply because the surface feels dry. Its shape, cleanliness, odor, and the condition of the materials around it still matter.

### The label is not the whole answer

How long the insulation was wet, whether the water was clean, how far it traveled, and what happened to the roof deck or ceiling below matter more than a simple material rule.

## Section 4

- **Eyebrow:** `LOOK BEYOND THE INSULATION`
- **H2:** `The Materials Around It May Need Attention Too`

Water may affect roof decking, framing, ceiling drywall, ducts, HVAC equipment, wiring, fixtures, baffles, and soffit areas. A ceiling stain may be the first clue, but it does not always show the full water path.

Looking at the insulation alone cannot tell you whether another part of the attic also needs attention. An inspection helps follow the moisture and identify which professional should handle each part.

---

## Section 5

- **Eyebrow:** `WHEN REMOVAL MAKES SENSE`
- **H2:** `Some Insulation Can Be Evaluated; Some Is Better Removed`

Removal is more likely when the insulation is soaked, matted, smelly, contaminated, visibly damaged, or preventing the materials underneath from being inspected.

It may also make sense when the material has lost its shape, water traveled through a large area, pest waste is mixed into it, the attic floor needs to be exposed for cleanup or air sealing, or nearby decking, ceiling, ducts, or wiring need attention.

A limited damp area found quickly may have a different answer. The important thing is not to close the attic back up before the source, the insulation, and the nearby materials have been properly evaluated.

---

## Section 6

- **Eyebrow:** `THE RIGHT ORDER`
- **H2:** `Repair First, Then Remove, Prepare, and Rebuild`

1. **Stop the moisture source.** Repair the roof leak, plumbing issue, condensate problem, exhaust problem, or other cause.
2. **See how far the water traveled.** Inspect the insulation and the roof deck, framing, ceiling, ducts, wiring, and equipment that can be seen.
3. **Bring in the right professional.** Roofing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, mold, structural, or water-restoration work may need to happen first.
4. **Remove material that should not remain.** Take out insulation that is saturated, matted, smelly, contaminated, or too damaged to provide a good starting point.
5. **Prepare the exposed attic.** Complete the cleanup, air sealing, baffles, ventilation preparation, and other attic work needed before insulation goes back in.
6. **Install fresh insulation when the attic is ready.** The new material should be the final layer over a dry, repaired, properly prepared space.

---

## Section 7

- **Eyebrow:** `WHEN MOISTURE AND PESTS OVERLAP`
- **H2:** `Wet Insulation With Pest Contamination Needs a More Complete Reset`

Moisture does not wash away droppings, urine, nesting material, guano, animal remains, or pest odor. Drying does not make contaminated insulation clean again.

When pest contamination is confirmed, the active pest or wildlife issue should be handled first. The old insulation can then be removed, the attic can be cleaned and prepared, entry points can be addressed, and fresh insulation can be installed when the space is ready.

## Section 8

- **Eyebrow:** `WHEN ANOTHER PROFESSIONAL IS NEEDED`
- **H2:** `Some Wet-Attic Conditions Need Specialized Help`

If the attic may contain vermiculite, do not disturb it like ordinary insulation. EPA advises homeowners to assume vermiculite may contain asbestos and to use a qualified asbestos professional when removal is necessary.

Visible growth, widespread water damage, unsafe framing, damaged electrical components, contaminated water, or moisture inside HVAC equipment may also require another specialist.

The homeowner does not have to solve the sequence alone. One professional can correct the source or specialized condition, and the attic-insulation work can follow when the space is ready.

## FAQ section

- **Eyebrow:** `FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS`
- **H2:** `Frequently Asked Questions About Wet Attic Insulation`

### Can wet attic insulation be left to dry?

Sometimes a small area found quickly may be evaluated after the source is corrected. Insulation that is soaked, matted, smelly, contaminated, visibly damaged, or uneven is more likely to need removal.

### Should new insulation be added over a wet area?

No. Correct the moisture source and make sure the attic is ready before new insulation is installed. Covering wet material can hide the problem and make future inspection harder.

### Does wet fiberglass always need to be removed?

Not automatically. The source, duration, cleanliness of the water, condition of the insulation, and the materials around it all matter. Fiberglass that is dirty, matted, smelly, contaminated, or no longer holding its shape is a poor candidate to remain.

### Can Good Attic diagnose mold or certify that the attic is dry?

Good Attic does not provide mold diagnoses or formal moisture clearance. We can document what is visible and coordinate the insulation work with the appropriate professional.

### What happens if the wet insulation is vermiculite?

Do not disturb suspected vermiculite like ordinary insulation. EPA advises homeowners to assume it may contain asbestos and involve a qualified asbestos professional when removal is necessary.
